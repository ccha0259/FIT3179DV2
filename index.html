01

THE WORKFORCE

Does Australia have enough dentist?

National dentist availability rose from 56.5 to 62.1 full-time-equivalent dentists per 100,000 people between 2014 and 2024. The state-level picture still varies: in 2024, the rate ranged from 35.4 in the Northern Territory to 66.9 in the ACT.

02

HOSPITAL CARE

A closer look at care in hospital.

Australia recorded 65,194 potentially preventable dental hospitalisations in 2014–15 and 89,181 in 2024–25. Comparing state counts shows the volume of hospital care. Population-based rates reveal another part of the picture.

i Larger populations can generate more admissions. These counts describe hospital activity; they do not measure an individual’s risk of hospitalisation.

AGE MAKES A DIFFERENCE

Children aged 5–9 stand out.

This age group had the highest dental hospitalisation rate in every year shown.

THE PROCEDURE MIX

Oral surgery accounts for the largest share.

40.7% of the 491,275 hospital dental procedures recorded in 2024–25 were oral surgery. One admission can involve more than one procedure.

03

CHILDREN’S SUBSIDISED CARE

What does children’s dental care provide?

The Child Dental Benefits Schedule (CDBS) subsidises basic dental services for eligible children. Its 5,286,544 recorded services in 2024 offer a view of the care provided through the program.

START WITH THE MIX

A different mix
in every state.

Each donut shows how a state’s CDBS services are divided among diagnostic, preventive, restorative and other services.

Compare the proportions, then hover to see the published rates behind them.

Equal-sized donuts show approximate service shares. They do not represent numbers of children.

LOOK BEYOND THE SHARE

Similar prevention rates.
Different treatment rates.

Queensland and South Australia recorded similar preventive service rates in 2024. Queensland’s restorative rate was higher: 2,195 compared with 1,716 services per 100,000 population.

This is an association between service rates. Eligibility, participation and treatment needs can all affect the pattern.

FROM CATEGORIES TO SERVICES

Nearly half of CDBS services were preventive.

Preventive services accounted for 48.2% of the national total in 2024. Follow the ribbons to see the detailed services within each category.

04

ACCESS & AGE

Who still faces barriers to care?

Having services available is one part of access. Among people aged 15+ who needed dental care in 2024–25, 13.8% made no visit at all, and 16.1% delayed or did not use care with cost a reason.

YOUNGER ADULTS

Around 22% of those aged 25–34 and 35–44 reported cost as a reason for delay or non-use.

OLDER ADULTS

Among people aged 75–84 who needed care, 8.7% were placed on a public dental waiting list.

AMONG THOSE WHO RECEIVED CARE

Visit frequency also differs by age.

The next chart considers people who needed to and saw a dental professional. Two to three visits made up a larger share in older age groups.

READING THE WHOLE PICTURE

Availability. Treatment. Access.

Workforce rates show capacity, hospital data describe activity and survey results reveal barriers. Read together, these measures help explain why the experience of dental care varies across Australia.
